MSSQL store hosting the centralized DB_ADMIN database (QuotationsInProgress + QuotationsStatus). Required for the In Progress page.

Inventory Time

Break timeout: a gap larger than this between two recounts is treated as a break (not counted), starting a new session. Isolated recount: time credited for a lone single-item session. Both in minutes.

Checked Orders

Slow order threshold: orders whose check duration exceeds this many minutes are highlighted in red and floated to the top of the Checked Orders results. In the summary totals they're counted as an estimate of seconds per product × products rather than their inflated real time (the table still shows the real time).

Cost Lookup Store (S2S)

MSSQL store used to look up product cost (UnitPriceC / UnitCost) by barcode. Powers cost columns in Shopify Sales and the In Progress price column.

    Setup

    All-history run: this traces every customer the store has ever had, and may take many minutes.
    How this works

    Window. Cancelled and fully refunded orders are ignored everywhere in this report. History from is optional — leave it blank and the report covers each store’s entire Shopify history. It only decides how far back to look; it does not decide who counts as lost.

    Counts as lost after. A customer is lost once they have gone this long without a completed order, measured from today in each store’s own timezone. Because the rule is a duration rather than a fixed date, every month on the chart is judged by the same standard — which a single cutoff date cannot do, since it leaves recent months needing only weeks of silence while older ones needed years. The most recent months cannot be judged yet and are shown as a shaded zone.

    Only customers acquired in this window. Off by default, so the departures cover everyone — including long-standing customers, who are the ones a churn report usually cares about most. Turn it on to narrow to customers whose very first completed order falls on or after the history date; anyone already buying before it is then excluded from the table and from every total.

    Another account. Two passes against every active Shopify store, including the customer’s own: first by email, then by first name + last name + ZIP. The second catches a re-registration, which Shopify forces onto a different email. Anyone who bought elsewhere within the silence period of going quiet here did not leave the business, and is excluded from every figure.

    Min. orders. 1 includes one-time buyers. Raise it to focus on repeat customers who stopped. Cancelled and refunded orders do not count towards it.

    Where new customers came from. Optional, and slower, because it runs the same two passes over everyone acquired in the window rather than just the leavers. It answers whether a “new” customer was already yours somewhere else — measured on one store, only half of them were genuinely new to the business. On an all-history run this covers the whole customer base, so it is slow enough to warn about.

    Sync latest Shopify data first. Before the report runs, every store with a local sync pulls whatever changed in Shopify since its last sync — usually a few seconds per store. Turn it off to run instantly on the data as of each store’s last sync. Stores that have never been synced are unaffected either way; they always use the live Shopify API.

    Reading the chart. Click any month to narrow the table, the totals and every analysis below to the customers who left in it.

    Shopify Data Sync

    Downloads each store's full customer and order history (statuses, tags, notes, line items, fulfillments) into the local database. Once a store is synced, the Lost Customers report runs from local data — fast and independent of the Shopify API. Sync fetches only what changed since the last run; Full resync re-downloads everything and removes records deleted in Shopify.
